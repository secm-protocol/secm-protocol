# RISK-01 — Northeast Brazil: Comprehensive Risk Map, July 2026 → December 2027

Compiled 2026-07-31. Risks ranked by **mechanism strength**, not by news salience.
Sources: NOAA/INPE/INMET, Ministério da Saúde, Fiocruz/InfoDengue, ONS, MJSP/Polícia
Federal reporting. **Analysis, not advice.**

---

## TIER 1 — Named mechanism, dated, high confidence

### R1. Hydrological — drought (the driver of almost everything below)
**Mechanism:** ENSO ocean–atmosphere coupling. NOAA: **82%** El Niño formation, **96%**
persistence Dec 2026–Feb 2027, **63% probability of a very strong event (≥2.0 °C)** in
the Nov–Jan trimester. Documented Northeast impact: reduced rainfall, water stress,
increased fire, smoke, degraded air quality.
**Peak window:** October 2026 – January 2027.
**Already visible:** São Luís entered rationing on Itapecuru river level decline; 50+
neighbourhoods simultaneously without supply in Jan 2026; 15+ day outages recorded.

### R2. Health — arbovirus explosion (the highest-magnitude human risk)
**Official status:** Ministério da Saúde issued a formal alert (Nota Informativa
nº 10/2026) on Super El Niño 2026/2027 raising dengue and chikungunya risk.
**Fiocruz/InfoDengue projection: ~1.7 million dengue cases in Brazil in 2027.**

**The cruel double mechanism — this is the part most coverage misses:**
1. El Niño temperature and humidity conditions favour *Aedes aegypti* proliferation
2. **And the drought itself manufactures the habitat** — when piped supply fails,
   households store water in open containers, creating breeding sites at scale

Water scarcity and mosquito-borne epidemic are not two separate risks. **The first
causes the second.** Peripheries with the worst supply reliability carry the highest
exposure.

### R3. Fire and air quality
Explicitly named in the official El Niño impact assessment: intensified drought →
water stress → **queimadas, smoke, degraded air quality**. Consequence: respiratory
morbidity, especially in children and elderly, concentrated in the same dry window
(Oct 2026 – Jan 2027). Caatinga and Cerrado transition zones most exposed.

### R4. Security — organised crime consolidation
**The structural fact:** **46 of Brazil's 88 mapped criminal organisations operate in
the Northeast.** PCC, Comando Vermelho and TCP operate in alliances across at least 17
states, per Federal and Civil Police investigations.

**Why the Northeast specifically:** the coast became an **export route for drugs to
Europe** and a **money-laundering platform through real estate**. This is a change in
function, not merely in volume — the region shifted from diffuse criminality to
strategic target.

**Amplifiers in this window:** territorial disputes with local groups intensify
violence; an election year concentrates political and territorial contestation; and
economic stress expands the recruitment pool.

### R5. Energy cost
**Current state is better than assumed:** Northeast reservoirs at **85.8%** (July
2026) — healthy. **The risk is forward-looking.** ONS warns that unfavourable hydrology
could end the wet period with reduced storage. Tariff exposure: red flag level 2 would
imply roughly **+12% on electricity tariffs**.
**Real window:** the 2027 wet season, following a weak Oct 2026–Jan 2027 rainy onset.

## TIER 2 — Strong mechanism, magnitude uncertain

### R6. Food inflation with no monetary room
El Niño peaks on the **soy planting window in MATOPIBA** (Oct 2026 – Jan 2027).
Simultaneously, **Selic sits at 14.25%** — already restrictive, with limited room to
ease if food prices spike. Food inflation hits the lowest-income population hardest,
and the Northeast has Brazil's highest concentration of it.

### R7. Rural exodus / internal migration
Historically documented Northeast drought response. Mechanism: crop failure and water
scarcity → rural-to-urban movement → pressure on already-underserved urban peripheries
— **the same peripheries carrying the highest arbovirus and security exposure.**

### R8. Administrative discontinuity
Elections 4 Oct 2026; new state and municipal administrations take office **January
2027** — precisely at the El Niño peak. Contract review, programme discontinuity and
personnel turnover arrive exactly when emergency response capacity is most needed.

---

## TIER 3 — Real but slower mechanisms

### R9. Sanitation deficit (risk amplifier, not standalone)
Chronic supply and sewage gaps multiply every health risk above. It is the coefficient
that turns a drought into an epidemic.

### R10. Coastal erosion and sea-level pressure
Real mechanism, decadal timescale. Not a 2026–2027 event risk, but affects coastal
infrastructure siting decisions being made now.

## THE COMPOUND SCENARIO — the finding that matters most

No single risk above is unprecedented. **The danger is that they arrive together in the
same 90-day window and amplify one another:**

```
Drought (Oct 26 – Jan 27)
   ↓ household water storage
Arbovirus epidemic (peak 2027, ~1.7M cases projected nationally)
   ↓
Crop failure in MATOPIBA → food inflation
   ↓ with Selic at 14.25%, no monetary response available
Economic stress → rural exodus → periphery pressure
   ↓ the same territory where factions recruit and contest
   ↓
All of it landing on administrations that took office in January 2027
```

**Every link in that chain has a named mechanism and a date.** That is what separates
this from speculation — and what makes the window forecastable rather than merely
worrying.

**The states with highest combined exposure** are those simultaneously in the
drought-impact zone, with fragile water systems, high faction contestation and large
underserved peripheries. Maranhão sits in that intersection: São Luís is already
rationing, and the state is inside both the drought zone and the MATOPIBA corridor.

## What would falsify this map

- El Niño failing to reach strong intensity (37% probability by NOAA's own figure)
- Northeast rainfall performing above forecast in the Dec–Mar wet season
- Dengue cases in 2027 landing materially below the ~1.7M national projection
- Reservoir levels holding above 60% through 2027
