## Political layer

Governor race: **Eduardo Braide (PSD)**, sitting mayor of São Luís, leads Quaest by
10+ points; Paraná Pesquisas shows a technical tie — Braide 34.6% vs **Orleans Brandão
(MDB)** 30.3%. Brandão launched with **182 of 217 municipal mayors** present — the
machine vote. Senate: sitting governor Carlos Brandão (PSB) and Weverton (PDT) lead.

**Under-priced structural consequence:** if Braide wins the governorship, São Luís
loses its mayor mid-term and the vice assumes in Jan 2027. Municipal contracts,
priorities and supplier relationships reset **twice** in one cycle (state + city).
Vendor registration and relationship-building windows open in Q4 2026, not later.

## The binding constraint (highest-value finding)

The **Estreito dos Mosquitos bridge** is the only road access to São Luís island, which
hosts the Itaqui and Ponta da Madeira port complex. One roadway is interdicted
indefinitely; DNIT is tendering a replacement structure. Daily traffic exceeds **11,000
vehicles**, largely heavy cargo bound for the port.

**The collision:** port capacity is expanding (+8M tons/yr from Berço 98; R$1.3bn in
terminal investment; concession renewed to 2051) while its single road artery is
degraded. Structural mismatch between growing throughput and a fragile access point.

Opportunity surface this creates: mainland-side warehousing and staging yards;
rail-first and cabotage logistics that bypass road dependency; heavy-vehicle
maintenance near the queue points; any service monetising truck dwell time.

## Economic engine

Itaqui: 4th largest public port in Brazil, record **36.8M tons in 2025**, concession
renewed through **2051** (Jan 2026) — the stability that unlocked capital. Two terminal
area auctions planned for 2026 (~R$1.3bn, agricultural and mineral bulk). VLI investing
R$80M in TPSL (completion Q1-2027).

**Rail corridor — the non-obvious vector:** ~R$1.3bn for rail modernisation;
Transnordestina revitalising ~400 km from São Luís to the Piauí border, plus a **new
grain terminal in Caxias**. Capital is being wired into the *interior corridor*
(Caxias/Timon axis), not only the capital. Land and logistics along that corridor are
where the infrastructure is arriving, and it attracts less attention than São Luís.

## Tourism — the clearest dated window

Lençóis Maranhenses: **UNESCO World Heritage since 2024**. State tourism **+60% since
2022**; ~90,000 tourists landed in São Luís in June 2025 alone, highest in 13 years.
Equatorial invested ~R$9.5M in Lençóis electrical infrastructure in H1-2026.

**2026-10-26 changes the customer profile, not just the volume.** Direct Lisbon service
means European arrivals with European service expectations, longer stays and
higher-value spend — arriving into a city whose hospitality stock was built for
domestic tourism. That gap is the opportunity: EU-standard receptive operations,
multilingual guiding, quality short-stay inventory near the Centro Histórico, and
Lençóis packaging sold in Europe rather than resold locally.

The window to prepare closes **before** October, not after.

## Urban growth vectors

São Luís residential prices rose **~14% in 2025** — 4th highest among Brazilian
capitals (behind Salvador, João Pessoa, Vitória). Official expansion vectors:
**Araçagi and Jardim Atlântico**.

Spillover logic: with capital-city prices up 14%, the island's other three
municipalities — **São José de Ribamar, Paço do Lumiar, Raposa** — absorb displaced
demand. These are separate municipalities on the same island: same commute, different
tax and land-price regime.

Chronic bottleneck: water supply dependency on the Italuís system, and a municipal
water/sewage plan being adapted to the new national sanitation framework. Sanitation
gaps in a growing city are a recurring source of contracted works.

## Religious layer — read structurally

Maranhão remains strongly Catholic (**64.4%**, above the national average), with
evangelical share advancing (17.2% in the 2010 baseline, +8.2 points in a decade).
Evangelical growth poles concentrate in **Cidade Operária, Itaqui-Bacanga and
Coroadinho** — dense peripheries where churches function as the effective social
infrastructure (family assistance, education, community organisation) and where
political mobilisation runs through pastoral conventions.

**Structural reading (not a religious judgement):** in these neighbourhoods the churches
are the **trust and distribution layer**. Dense, underserved consumer markets with an
existing organised network. Any service reaching them does so through that layer or not
at all. Electorally, the same structure makes these districts the most contested in the
October race.

## Cross-references nobody runs

1. **Port expansion × degraded single access** — throughput growing into a fragile
   artery. Bottleneck is dated and public.
2. **Election × double administrative reset** — state changes hands, and the capital
   likely changes mayor mid-term. Two procurement resets in one cycle.
3. **Lisbon route × domestic-grade hospitality stock** — new customer profile arriving
   into unprepared supply, with a fixed date.
4. **Rail capital × interior corridor** — money flowing to Caxias/Timon axis while
   attention stays on the capital.
5. **Capital price appreciation × neighbouring municipalities** — same island, same
   commute, different price regime.
